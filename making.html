How Storm Chamber was made

1 · Raw data

The file is polar, and it is not a picture

A WSR-88D radar writes rays: for each azimuth, a list of range gates, each with a reflectivity in dBZ and a correlation coefficient. This file, KDDC20130519_000455_V06, is a public volume scan from Dodge City. The generator keeps gates with ρhv ≥ 0.8 unless they are at least 40 dBZ, removes isolated patches, and resamples each sweep onto a regular azimuth × range grid.

2 · Geometry

A map hides fourteen cones

The antenna tilts through fourteen elevations, and each beam bends with the atmosphere while the ground curves away. Both are in the standard 4/3-Earth model, implemented once in src/storm/beam.js. The JavaScript matches the Python generator to within 5 m on every sweep. The plot here is drawn live from the piece's own storm.json by that same module.

3 · Volume

Between the cones, nothing was measured

Each voxel center in an 80 × 80 × 20 km box is converted to slant range, azimuth and elevation. It takes the nearest gate from the two sweeps that bracket it, interpolated linearly in dBZ, and is left empty below the lowest beam and above the highest sweep that reaches it. A light Gaussian (0.3–0.35 km) removes stair steps. The scan took 4.5 minutes, but the box treats it as one instant, and the chapter before it says so.

5 · Why this method

Why not a mesh, or real cloud physics?

An isosurface would have implied that a storm has a surface. Real multiple scattering would have cost the frame rate for light that is a rendering choice anyway. So the sun, at its true position for 7:05 p.m., is baked once on the CPU into a light texture: optical depth walked slice by slice away from the sun. Each ray step then reads that value, instead of marching a second ray.

6 · Performance

Dropped frames, not average frames

A 4 × 4 × 4 occupancy grid lets rays skip empty space. Under vsync an average frame time hides trouble, since a third of frames at 33 ms still averages 22 ms. So the loop counts frames that miss the display interval: over 5% in a second steps the pixel ratio down by 15% and bars it from returning for 30 s, and three clean seconds step it back up. At the lowest ratio the ray step coarsens too.

7 · The line

What is real, and what is not

The piece puts this on screen in the chapter that fills the box. Reflectivity is not cloud density, and it is not wind: the updraft in the section chapter is inferred from the shape of the echo.

Measured
The fourteen sweeps. The strongest echo, 68.5 dBZ, and the echo tops are read from them directly, not from the grid.
Interpolated
Everything between the sweeps, in dBZ. Fine structure smaller than the spacing is smoothed or lost.
Recorded
The tornado: NOAA Storm Events, event 457695. The radar did not see it; the lowest beam passed 900 m above that ground.
Drawn
The light, the shadow and the glow of strong echo. Heights are doubled until you ask for true scale.

8 · Trade-offs

What was given up

Softness is the price of honesty here: at a 0.5 km grid, sharper edges would be invented. The horizontal bands high in the storm are the sweeps themselves, and they stay. The NWS colors are loud, but they are what a forecaster reads, so they are one toggle away, not the default.

The other three, briefly

  • The Shadow of Water

    Patches and coexistence walls are built from tables computed in advance with IAPWS formulations. Shading follows density, and the scribed lines are real isotherms, isobars and density contours, computed in the fragment shader with fwidth.

  • The Electric Heart

    The samples live in a texture and the vertex shader moves each point between the strip and the loop, so the fold is one continuous mesh. Trails are computed from sample time, not accumulated between frames.

  • Between Tides

    The ground and the sea share a height texture. The waterline is simply where the fragment's elevation meets the measured level, so scrubbing the month only updates a few uniforms.
