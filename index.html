Datascape

Every chart is flat because something was folded away.

Four stories begin with a chart you already know: a radar map, a phase diagram, an ECG, a tide curve. Each opens into the space it flattened. Every surface is measured or computed from public scientific data.

Stories

Four guided pieces in eight chapters each. Every one starts flat, and every one can reach its reveal in one step or be read from the first page.

  • Story I

    Storm Chamber

    Weather radar map → the storm it was measured on

    One volume scan of the Dodge City radar, thirteen minutes before the 2013 Rozel tornado: fourteen cones of measurement, filled into a box of sky you can cut open.

    NOAA NEXRAD Level II · ray-marched volume

  • Story II

    The Shadow of Water

    Phase diagram → the surface that casts it

    The textbook diagram of ice, liquid and vapor is a shadow. This is the object that casts it: every stable state of water in pressure, temperature and density.

    IAPWS-95 via CoolProp · nothing exaggerated

  • Story III

    The Electric Heart

    12-lead ECG + Frank XYZ → the loop of the heart's vector

    Twelve traces on red paper, and three more leads recorded beside them. Plot those three against each other, fold time away, and each heartbeat becomes a loop in space.

    PTB Diagnostic ECG, PhysioNet

  • Story IV

    Between Tides

    Tide gauge curve → the shore it floods

    A month of measured sea level at Eastport, Maine, carried sideways onto the surveyed ground of the South Lubec flats, where seven meters of tide uncover square kilometers of seabed.

    NOAA CO-OPS · CUDEM · a level, not a flood model

Beauty answers to the data

Measured
Every story says which parts were measured, which were interpolated, and which are a rendering choice, on screen, at its big moment.
Reproducible
Each story's data rebuilds byte for byte from its public sources, and CI checks that every week.
Inspectable
Figures in the prose are tested against the data. See how Storm Chamber was made.

Built with Three.js and Vite, without a framework. Each piece's “About the data” panel names its sources and limits.
